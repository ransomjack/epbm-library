## Crane (2020): stakeholder trust at a glance

| Concept | Detail |
|---|---|
| Trust definition | A stakeholder's willingness to be **vulnerable** to a business (Pirson & Malhotra, 2011) |
| 3 core trust dimensions | Ability (can deliver) · Benevolence (genuinely cares) · Integrity (fair and honest) |
| 3 supplementary signals | Voluntary action · Transparency · Responsiveness |
| Voluntary action signals | **Benevolence.** Acting without being forced shows genuine care |
| Transparency signals | **Integrity.** Nothing to hide = trustworthy |
| Proposition 1 | Your action toward **one** stakeholder sends trust cues to **all** others |
| Proposition 6 (key) | **Prioritising** stakeholders **decreases** trust. Signals favouritism and zero-sum |
| Freeman (1984) | If a court or government forces you to fix a problem = managerial failure |
| Proximity (P5) | The closer a stakeholder feels (social/cultural/physical), the stronger the trust/distrust effect |

## PEST variations: know all five

| Framework | Adds to PEST | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| PEST | — | Political · Economic · Social · Technological (baseline) |
| PESTEL / PESTLE | + Environmental + Legal | Adds ecology and legal compliance |
| STEEPLE | + Environmental + Legal + Ethics | Takes a CSR perspective. Remember this |
| STEEPLED | + Environmental + Legal + Ethics + Demographics | Most comprehensive. Adds Demographics |
| STEER | + Regulatory factors | Driven by environmental awareness |

**PEST in SWOT:** PEST gives you the O and T (Opportunities and Threats). Strengths and Weaknesses come from internal analysis.

## Bozonelos & Tsagdis (2023): geopolitical resilience at a glance

| Concept | Detail |
|---|---|
| Geopolitics definition | How geography affects politics and the relations among states (Caldara & Iacoviello, 2022) |
| LIO | Liberal International Order: built by the US and allies after WWII on free trade and multilateral institutions |
| 3 phases in IB | Emerging (pre-2008) → Approval (2008–2019, doubled) → Expansion (2019–present) |
| 6 levels of analysis | Individual → Firm → Sub-national → National → Supra-national → Global |
| Inside-In view | Fix internal processes only. Ignores the geopolitical iceberg. Titanic deckchairs. |
| Inside-Out view | Improve the firm's connection to the external environment (supply chain, volatile nodes) |
| Outside-In view | Make visible what is **beyond** the firm's control; build locational portfolio resilience |
| Outside-Out view | The ultimate geopolitics view. Resilience beyond any country's political economy. Under-practised. |
| Bouncing-with (key) | **Pre-emptive** viability: anticipate and manage so you never suffer the big setback |
| Fat tail events | Unexpected but **not** rare, just underweighted by conventional risk models (e.g. Russia invasion) |
| Turkey analogy | Turkey fed for 1,000 days feels safe, then Thanksgiving. Western MNEs in Russia = the turkey. |
| 6 resilience dimensions | Reputational · Organisational · Financial · Technological · Operational · Business Model |

## Exam traps: don't fall for these

Prof. Abraham's MCQs are specifically designed to test whether you know the difference between very similar-sounding concepts. These are the most common mistakes students make. Read every one of these before the exam.

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Regulatory Agencies ≠ Government</p>

They are **two separate** stakeholder groups in the PPT. If an option says they are the same group, it is wrong.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">ESG: E = Environmental, not Economic</p>

In PEST, E = Economic. In ESG, E = Environmental. Do not mix these up. A very common trap in multiple forms.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">PEST E = Economic, not Environmental</p>

Environmental is only added in PESTEL/PESTLE. The base PEST has Political, **Economic**, Social, Technological.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Crane's P6: prioritisation destroys trust</p>

Seems counter-intuitive. Surely focusing on important stakeholders is good? No. By picking winners, you signal to everyone else that they might not get what they need. It damages ability, benevolence, and integrity simultaneously.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Voluntary action signals benevolence (not integrity)</p>

Transparency → **integrity**. Voluntary action → **benevolence**. Do not swap these. MCQ options are specifically designed to exploit this confusion.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Africa = highest rate, not highest value</p>

Africa had the highest **growth rate** (+75%). Developed economies had the highest **absolute value** (USD 867 BN). The exam tests this distinction explicitly.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Cultural Risk is not one of the six country risks</p>

The six are: Political, Economic, Transfer, Exchange Rate, Location/Neighbourhood, Sovereign. Cultural Risk will appear as a trap option.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Bouncing-with is pre-emptive, not reactive</p>

All other types (back, above, beyond) assume you already suffered a setback. Bouncing-**with** means you anticipated and managed so you never suffered the big hit in the first place.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Inside-In = Titanic deckchairs</p>

Fixing internal processes while ignoring massive external geopolitical forces. Perfect internal optimisation + geopolitical blindness = rearranging deckchairs on the Titanic.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">AUKUS = Australia, UK, United States</p>

Not Japan, not Canada, not Singapore. Australia, United Kingdom, United States. Trilateral security partnership. A precision fact the exam loves.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Disintermediation vs re-intermediation</p>

Disintermediation: technology **removes** old middlemen (travel agents gone). Re-intermediation: technology **creates** new middlemen (Amazon, eBay, Expedia). Amazon is **not** disintermediation; it is re-intermediation.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Six levels of analysis ≠ six resilience dimensions</p>

Levels of analysis (Figure 2): Individual, Firm, Sub-national, National, Supra-national, Global. McKinsey resilience dimensions: Reputational, Organisational, Financial, Technological, Operational, Business Model. Do not confuse these two completely different lists.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">India-China trade deficit = USD 112.60 billion in 2025-26</p>

The exam may offer USD 80B, USD 90B, or USD 145B as distractors. Know the precise number cold.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Tax avoidance is not a motivation for FDI</p>

The four PPT motivations are: Resources, Markets, Efficiency, and Strategic Assets. Tax avoidance and competitive retaliation are common fake fifth options.

</div>

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">"Born Global" firms go international from near the very beginning</p>

They do **not** spend their first decade building domestic strength. They internationalise at a much faster rate than traditional multinationals.

</div>
