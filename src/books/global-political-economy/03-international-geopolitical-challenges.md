## Three geopolitical events increasing business risk

Prof. Abraham's PPT identifies exactly three triggers. MCQs will test whether you know the precise three: not the GFC, not Brexit, not climate change alone.

| Event | What it is | Business impact |
|---|---|---|
| ① New nationalism and trade wars | Governments prioritising domestic industry, imposing barriers, screening FDI | Higher tariffs, tighter Rules of Origin, investment restrictions, regulatory hurdles for M&A and R&D |
| ② Covid-19 pandemic | Lockdowns, factory closures, worker movement restrictions | Exposed fragility of lean Just-in-Time supply chains; demand volatility; accelerated digital shift |
| ③ Regional conflicts | Armed conflicts (Ukraine, Gaza, and future flashpoints) disrupting commodity markets and supply routes | Price volatility in critical inputs, investment uncertainty, rising capital costs |

## New nationalism and trade wars

This is the most content-dense area of the PPT. Each sub-theme is a separate MCQ candidate.

- **Growing nationalist sentiment:** investment screening by governments, domestic investment promotion, emphasis on national resilience, regulatory hurdles for M&A, FDI, and R&D.
- **Increase in trade restrictions:** higher tariffs (US tariffs on Chinese goods), withdrawal of trade concessions (US GSP withdrawal for India in 2019), tighter Rules of Origin.
- **Uncertainties of trade with China:** growth declining from ~10% to ~5%; India-China trade deficit at USD 112.60 billion in 2025-26 (despite tensions); chip wars (US restricting semiconductors to China, with Taiwan/TSMC at the centre); technology decoupling; 5G (Huawei exclusion pressure).
- **Weakening of the WTO:** the US is blocking Appellate Body appointments, so the dispute settlement "court" is non-functional, and bilateral/unilateral measures fill the vacuum.

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Two numbers to memorise</p>

India-China trade deficit = **USD 112.60 billion** in 2025-26. GSP withdrawal for India = **2019**. The exam may offer USD 80B, USD 90B, or USD 145B as distractors. Know it cold.

</div>

## How have firms coped? Strategic responses

Four company examples are the most tested. Match each strategy to the right company:

| Strategy | What it means | Company example |
|---|---|---|
| Reshoring | Moving production out of high-risk geographies to home countries or trusted partners | **Komatsu:** diversified production out of China |
| Just-in-Case (JIC) | Replacing lean JIT inventory with safety stock buffers | **Apple** in India: building redundant manufacturing capacity outside China |
| Supplier digitisation | Building redundancy and using digital tools to improve supplier visibility | **ABB:** collaborated with and digitised supplier operations |
| Near-shoring | Moving production closer to home, but not fully back (to nearby trusted countries) | **Murata:** moved production from China to Thailand (ASEAN) |
| R&D / Automation | Using technology to reduce dependence on specific geographies | AI/ML for predictive supply chain capabilities |
| Technology substitution | Replacing hardware-dependent solutions with software alternatives | AI-powered analytics replacing physical inventory buffers |

Split into two dimensions: Trade & Economics (5 drivers) and Security (5 drivers). Know which belongs to which.

| Dimension | Value driver | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Trade & Economics | ① Trade agreements | USMCA product content requirements |
| Trade & Economics | ② Import/export controls | EU regulation on dual-use technologies |
| Trade & Economics | ③ Domestic policies (environment, labour, immigration) | Emissions regulations; USMCA wage requirements |
| Trade & Economics | ④ Tariffs and trade barriers | US tariffs on steel, aluminium, EVs from China |
| Trade & Economics | ⑤ Domestic industrial policies | India's Make in India initiative: subsidies |
| Security | ⑥ Foreign investment restrictions | EU screening for FDI in sensitive technologies |
| Security | ⑦ Sanctions, embargoes, restricted lists | China's Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law |

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">AUKUS</p>

Australia, United Kingdom, United States. **Not** Japan, Canada, or Singapore. It is a trilateral security and technology partnership (nuclear-powered submarines). It sits under "Multilateral cooperation and alliances" in the Security dimension.

## Bozonelos & Tsagdis (2023): geopolitical resilience in IB

**The core problem:** even after the GFC, Covid, and Russia-Ukraine, the treatment of geopolitics in international business has been "fragmented". The term appears mostly as an adjective, with very limited actionable guidance.

<div class="callout">
<p class="callout-title">Key definition</p>

Geopolitics (Caldara & Iacoviello, 2022): how geography affects politics and the relations among states. Shaped by geography, resources (natural and man-made), demographics, and international relations.

</div>

**Three historical phases of geopolitics in IB:**

| Phase | Period | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Emerging | Pre-2008 | Geopolitics barely discussed. The world was congruent with US hegemony and liberal values. |
| Approval | 2008–2019 | Geopolitics in IB publications **doubled**. Driven by the GFC, which shattered financial capitalism's dominance. |
| Expansion | 2019–present | Geopolitics becomes "part and parcel" of all IB: trade, supply chains, strategy, FDI. |

**Six levels of analysis (Figure 2):**

| Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| Individual | "Strong men" autocratic leaders whose personal decisions shape geopolitics (Putin, Xi) |
| Firm | MNEs, international joint ventures, strategic alliances |
| Sub-national | Clusters, entrepreneurial ecosystems (Silicon Valley, Bangalore tech cluster) |
| National | Varieties of capitalism, institutional configurations |
| Supra-national | Geo-regions, trading blocs (EU, ASEAN, RCEP), identity politics |
| Global | Spheres of influence: US sphere vs China sphere vs Russia sphere |

<div class="callout trap">
<p class="callout-title">Common trap</p>

MCQs often present four of the six levels and ask which is missing. The order is: Individual → Firm → Sub-national → National → Supra-national → Global. "Spheres of influence" = **Global** level (not National or Supra-national).

</div>

## The four-view framework for geopolitical resilience

This is the framework's most practically powerful tool. It shows four different ways a firm can position itself relative to geopolitical forces.

| View | Position | What it means | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside-In | Inside the organisation, looking in | Improve internal structures: optimise production lines, reduce bottlenecks. | Completely misses the geopolitical iceberg. Titanic deckchairs. |
| Inside-Out | Inside the organisation, looking out | Improve connection to the external environment: supply chain, volatile nodes. | Better, but still insufficient for large geopolitical hazards. |
| Outside-In | Outside the organisation, looking in | Make visible what is **beyond** the firm's control; build locational portfolio resilience. | Still reactive to country-level forces. |
| Outside-Out ★ | Outside the organisation, looking out | Resilience built **beyond** any country's political economy. Addresses existential geopolitical threats. | Under-practised in IB, but the gold standard. |

## Four actionable insights and "bouncing-with"

**The four actionable insights from Bozonelos & Tsagdis:**

1. **Treat geopolitics as continuous, not periodic.** Stop treating it as a shock that interrupts normal business. Treat it like global warming: always running in the background.
2. **Anticipate fat tail events.** These are events that appear unexpected but are **not** actually infrequent, just underweighted. The Russia-Ukraine invasion had ample prior evidence. Western MNEs in Russia ignored it.
3. **Transition from risk to resilience.** Risk analysis alone is not enough. Build genuine resilience using scenario planning, contrarian thinking, and risk insurance. Six McKinsey resilience dimensions: Reputational, Organisational, Financial, Technological, Operational, Business Model.
4. **Pursue multilevel analysis.** Consider all six levels simultaneously. Each dimension of resilience is multi-scalar.

**"Bouncing-with", the novel resilience concept:**

| Type | Meaning | Adequacy |
|---|---|---|
| Bouncing **back** | Returning to the pre-setback position | Limited. Just recovering the old position is not enough. |
| Bouncing **above** | Emerging stronger than before from the setback | Better but still reactive. Assumes a setback has occurred. |
| Bouncing **beyond** | Going further forward after the setback; transforming | Better still, but still assumes you suffered the setback first. |
| Bouncing **with** ★ | **Pre-emptive** viability: anticipate and manage so you **never** suffer the big setback | The gold standard. Continuous, proactive, endogenous. The paper's recommendation. |

<div class="callout">
<p class="callout-title">The turkey analogy (Taleb, 2010)</p>

A turkey is fed every day for 1,000 days and concludes it is safe and loved, until Thanksgiving arrives. Most Western MNEs in Russia were the turkey: 1,000 days of normal business, months of accumulating warning signals ignored, then completely blindsided when the invasion happened.

</div>
