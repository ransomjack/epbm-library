## New nationalism and trade wars

The densest part of the slides. Each point is a possible question.

- **Growing nationalism:** governments screen foreign investment, promote domestic investment, stress national resilience, and put hurdles in front of M&A, FDI and R&D.
- **More trade restrictions:** higher tariffs (US tariffs on Chinese goods), withdrawn concessions (the US ended India's GSP benefits in **2019**), tighter rules of origin.
- **Uncertain trade with China:** growth slowing from about 10% to about 5%; an India–China trade deficit of **USD 112.60 billion in 2025-26** despite political tension; chip wars, with the US restricting semiconductors to China and Taiwan's TSMC at the centre; technology decoupling; pressure to exclude Huawei from 5G.
- **A weaker WTO:** the US has blocked appointments to the Appellate Body, so the dispute-settlement "court" can't function, and countries fall back on bilateral or unilateral action.

## Bozonelos & Tsagdis (2023): geopolitical resilience

**The problem they address:** even after the financial crisis, Covid and Russia–Ukraine, international business research has treated geopolitics in a fragmented way. The word is mostly used as an adjective, with little practical guidance.

<div class="callout">
<p class="callout-title">Definition</p>

**Geopolitics** (Caldara & Iacoviello, 2022): how geography affects politics and relations between states. It is shaped by geography, natural and man-made resources, demographics and international relations.

</div>

## Four actionable insights

1. **Treat geopolitics as continuous, not occasional.** It is not a shock that interrupts normal business; like climate change, it is always running in the background.
2. **Anticipate fat-tail events.** They look unexpected but are not actually rare; conventional risk models just give them too little weight. Russia's invasion of Ukraine had plenty of warning signs, and Western firms in Russia ignored them.
3. **Move from risk to resilience.** Risk analysis is not enough. Build resilience with scenario planning, contrarian thinking and insurance, across McKinsey's six dimensions: reputational, organisational, financial, technological, operational and business model.
4. **Analyse at every level at once.** All six levels matter together, and every dimension of resilience plays out across them.

### "Bouncing with": the paper's big idea

<Fig caption="Illustrative shapes, not data. The dashed line is pre-setback performance; the vertical line marks the setback.">
  <Bounce />
</Fig>

The first three all assume the setback has already happened. **Bouncing with** means anticipating and managing so that the big hit never lands: continuous, proactive, built in.

<div class="callout">
<p class="callout-title">The turkey analogy (Taleb, 2010)</p>

A turkey is fed every day for 1,000 days and concludes that it is safe and loved, until Thanksgiving. Most Western firms in Russia were the turkey: years of normal business, months of warning signs ignored, then caught completely off guard by the invasion.

</div>

<p class="callout-title">Beyond the notes: questions a board can ask</p>

- Are we in the Inside-In box, polishing operations while ignoring the map?
