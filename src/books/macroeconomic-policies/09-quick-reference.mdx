## Formulas

| Formula | In words | Check it with |
|---|---|---|
| **Y = C + I + G + NX** | Output is bought by households, firms, the government and, net, foreigners | Imports are subtracted because C, I and G already include them |
| **Value added = sales − purchases from other firms** | GDP is the sum of value added, never of turnover | Sales 1,000, purchases 980: value added 20 |
| **Real growth ≈ nominal growth − inflation** | Growth in quantities only | 10.3% − 2.5% = 7.8% |
| **GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100** | The price index of everything produced at home | It has no fixed basket |
| **Inflation = change in CPI ÷ earlier CPI × 100** | How fast the basket is getting dearer | 114.3 to 117.1 is about 2.5% |
| **Real interest rate ≈ nominal rate − inflation** | What a saver gains in purchasing power | 4% − 6% = −2% |
| **Money multiplier = 1 ÷ reserve ratio; M = m × B** | Deposits created from each rupee of reserves | 10% gives 10: Rs. 100 becomes Rs. 1,000 |
| **Keynesian multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)** | Income created from each rupee of new spending | MPC 0.8 gives 5 |
| **Tax-cut multiplier = MPC ÷ (1 − MPC)** | Smaller, because part of a tax cut is saved first | MPC 0.8 gives 4 |
| **Budget balance = T − G** | Negative is a deficit | T 90, G 110: deficit of 20 |
| **Output gap = actual − potential output** | Positive is overheating, negative is slack | 104 against 100: plus 4 |
| **Net investment = investment − depreciation** | What is actually added to the capital stock | 100 − 30 = 70 |
| **M = P × Y** | With output fixed, money moves only prices | One bottle: money 100 to 200, price 100 to 200 |
| **Inflation = expected inflation − b × (u − natural rate) + shock** | The Phillips curve | A: 7% and 2%. B: 4% and 6% |
| **S − I = X − M** | A trade-deficit country is borrowing others' saving | India and the US borrow; China, Germany and Japan lend |
| **Real exchange rate = nominal rate × price abroad ÷ price at home** | The price of foreign goods in terms of home goods | 82 × 105,900 ÷ 71,00,000 = 1.22 |
| **Import cover = reserves ÷ monthly imports** | How long reserves could pay for imports | India: about 11 months |

## Pairs that are easy to confuse

| This | Is not this | The difference |
|---|---|---|
| GDP: where it is made | Who owns the maker | GDP counts what is produced on Indian soil, whoever owns it |
| Nominal | Real | Real has the price change taken out |
| CPI | WPI and the GDP deflator | CPI is what households pay, imports included. WPI is wholesale goods. The deflator covers everything made at home |
| Headline inflation | Core inflation | Core leaves out food and fuel |
| Disinflation | Deflation | Prices rising more slowly; prices falling |
| Repo rate | SDF and MSF | Banks borrow at the repo; park cash at the SDF (floor); borrow in emergencies at the MSF (ceiling) |
| Open market operation | QE and QT | Fine-tuning; large and lasting changes in the quantity of money |
| A rate hike | Quantitative tightening | Raises the price of money; cuts its quantity |
| CRR | SLR | Cash kept with the RBI; deposits held in safe government bonds |
| Monetary base (B) | Broad money (M) | Made by the central bank; includes deposits banks create by lending |
| Money multiplier | Keynesian multiplier | Multiplies deposits, leaks into reserves; multiplies income, leaks into saving |
| Monetary policy | Fiscal policy | Money and interest rates; spending and taxes |
| Movement along a curve | Shift of a curve | A price-level change moves you along; money, the budget or confidence moves the curve |
| Demand shock | Supply shock | Inflation and output move together; inflation up, output down |
| Frictional and structural unemployment | Cyclical unemployment | The natural rate; the part caused by weak demand |
| Stablecoin | CBDC and tokenised deposit | Private firm; central bank; commercial bank. Only the bank creates money by lending |
| Nominal exchange rate | Real exchange rate | Currency against currency; goods against goods. Trade follows the real one |
| Depreciation | Devaluation | The market does it to a floating currency; a government does it to a fixed one |
| Rupee invoice | Dollar invoice | A weaker rupee brings more volume; more margin |
| Transaction exposure | Translation and economic exposure | A payment due; a subsidiary's accounts; long-run competitive position |
| Forward | Option | Fixes the rate and gives up the upside; keeps the upside for a premium |

## Which way does it move?

| When this happens | This follows | And the result is |
|---|---|---|
| The RBI cuts the repo rate | Loans get cheaper, borrowing rises | Demand up; output up in the short run; inflation later |
| The RBI buys bonds or cuts the CRR | More reserves, or a bigger multiplier | More money, if banks choose to lend |
| Banks turn cautious | The multiplier falls; money is parked at the RBI | Easing does not reach borrowers |
| Government spends more or cuts taxes | Demand shifts right, multiplied through the rounds | Output up while prices are sticky; a deficit to finance |
| Money grows faster than output for long | Too much money chasing too few goods | Only prices rise |
| Prices start to fall | Buyers wait, real debts grow, firms cut wages | Demand falls further |
| Oil jumps, or the monsoon fails | Costs rise; supply shifts left | Inflation up and output down together |
| People expect more inflation | Wage demands and costs rise | The wage-price spiral |
| The central bank tightens to disinflate | Demand shifts left; output falls below potential | Inflation and expectations fall; output then recovers |
| The Fed raises its rate | Capital moves to dollar assets | Rupee weaker, reserves lower, imports dearer |
| The rupee depreciates in real terms | Indian goods become relatively cheaper | Exports up, imports down |
| A country fixes its exchange rate | It must sell reserves to defend it | Its interest rate follows the anchor country's |
| A tariff is imposed | Imported goods cost more at home | A supply shock at home; the trade gap still set by S − I |

## Easy mistakes

| The mistake | The right way to think |
|---|---|
| Adding up every firm's sales to get GDP | Only final goods count, or the value added at each stage |
| "Nominal GDP rose 10%, so we produced 10% more" | Part of it is prices |
| "Inflation fell, so prices fell" | Prices are still rising, more slowly |
| "The government sold bonds, so money was printed" | Money is created only when the central bank buys |
| "Banks lend out the deposits they receive" | Loans create deposits. Banks create money, not wealth |
| Assuming more reserves always means more lending | The multiplier belongs to the banks |
| Giving a tax cut the same multiplier as spending | Part of a tax cut is saved before the first round |
| "After a supply shock the central bank can fix both" | Its tool works on demand. It must choose |
| "The currency fell, so exports will rise" | Only if the fall is real |
| "The rupee rose to 95" | A bigger number of rupees per dollar is a weaker rupee |
| "A tariff will close the trade deficit" | The deficit equals investment minus saving |

## The module in twelve lines

1. GDP is the market value of final goods and services produced within a country in a year. It equals the sum of value added, and it is bought by C, I, G and NX.
2. Real GDP moves only with quantities. Nominal growth less inflation is real growth.
3. The RBI targets CPI inflation of 4%, within 2% to 6%. What a saver or borrower should care about is the real interest rate.
4. Money is whatever is accepted as payment. The rupee is backed by trust in the economy, and most money is created by banks when they lend.
5. The central bank controls the base and the price of money. The multiplier is in the hands of the banks.
6. Economies fluctuate around a potential set by capital, labour and technology. Policy manages the gap, not the trend.
7. Demand is steered by the budget and by money. Each rupee of new spending is multiplied, and aggregate demand slopes down.
8. In the short run prices are sticky and money raises output. In the long run it raises only prices. Falling prices are more dangerous than rising ones.
9. Supply slopes up only for given expectations. That is why central banks guard inflation expectations, and why disinflation hurts before it helps.
10. A supply shock raises inflation and lowers output together, and leaves the central bank a choice between them.
11. S − I = X − M: a deficit country borrows the world's saving. Trade follows the real exchange rate, so a weaker currency helps only if it is real.
12. The rupee floats under the RBI's management, the dollar still dominates, and a firm's protection is pricing power, hedging and not depending on any one thing.
