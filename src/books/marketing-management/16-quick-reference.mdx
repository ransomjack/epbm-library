## Formulas and number rules

Marketing has few formulas, and nearly all of them sit in two places: customer lifetime value and the arithmetic of a promotion. Each line has a number to check it with.

| Formula | In words | Check it with | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic CLV = annual revenue × loyal years × profit margin** | A first estimate that ignores time, acquisition cost and customers leaving | 5,000 × 20 × 10% = Rs. 10,000 | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **Present value = future amount / (1 + i)<sup>n</sup>** | Money due later is worth less today | Rs. 121 due in 2 years at 10% = Rs. 100 | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **CLV = sum of m<sub>t</sub> / (1 + i)<sup>t</sup>, less AC** | Discount each year's margin; subtract the acquisition cost | Rs. 500 for 20 years at 10% = 4,257; less 1,500 = Rs. 2,757 | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **CLV = sum of r<sup>t-1</sup> × m<sub>t</sub> / (1 + i)<sup>t</sup>, less AC** | Also multiply each year by the chance the customer is still there | At 80% retention: 1,664 less 1,500 = Rs. 164 | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **Survival in year t = r<sup>t-1</sup>** | The customer is certain in year 1 | At 50% retention: 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **Churn rate = 1 - retention rate** | Lost and kept customers add to 100% | 80% retained means 20% churn | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **Discount rate: use 10% if finance gives none** | Firms borrow at roughly 8% to 15% |  | [Ch. 3](/marketing-management/what-a-customer-is-worth/#calculating-customer-lifetime-value) |
| **Effective discount of "x% more" = x / (100 + x)** | Extra quantity is a smaller discount than it sounds | 33% more = 25% off; 25% more = 20% off; 50% more = 33% off | [Ch. 4](/marketing-management/how-buyers-decide/#framing-a-promotion-33-more-or-33-off) |
| **Price per unit with a bonus = old price / (1 + extra share)** | Always compare offers per unit | 1.33 litres for Rs. 100 = Rs. 75 a litre | [Ch. 4](/marketing-management/how-buyers-decide/#framing-a-promotion-33-more-or-33-off) |
| **Euclidean distance = square root of the sum of squared differences** | How far apart two respondents' ratings are | (6, 6, 5) and (7, 7, 5): root of 1 + 1 + 0 = 1.41 | [Ch. 8](/marketing-management/segmentation/#how-to-segment-judgement-or-cluster-analysis) |
| **Positioning = target segment + differentiation** | Whom you speak to, and what you offer that rivals do not |  | [Ch. 10](/marketing-management/positioning/#what-positioning-is) |
| **Contribution = price - variable cost** | Keep a brand whose contribution is positive, even if it shows a loss after fixed costs | 100 - 70 = 30, though allocated fixed cost is 40 | [Ch. 13](/marketing-management/brand-extension-and-portfolio/#brand-portfolio-rationalisation) |
| **80/20 rule** | About 20% of a firm's brands bring about 80% of sales or profit |  | [Ch. 13](/marketing-management/brand-extension-and-portfolio/#brand-portfolio-rationalisation) |

## Easy to confuse

Pairs of ideas that sound alike, each separated in one line.

| This | Is not this | The difference |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing** | Selling or advertising | Marketing starts from the customer's need and covers all four Ps. Selling starts from the product; advertising is one part of promotion. |
| **Push** | Pull | Push works on the customer (the door-to-door salesman). Pull works because the customer already wants it (asking for a brand by name). |
| **Customer** | Consumer | The customer buys; the consumer uses. A mother buys the cereal her child eats. |
| **Decision** | Activity | "Which distributor to appoint" is a decision. Market research and revenue tracking are activities that feed decisions. |
| **Place** | Market selection | Place means channels. Choosing which market to enter is targeting, part of STP. |
| **STP** | The 4Ps | STP is strategy: long-term and hard to change. The 4Ps are tactics that must serve it. |
| **Intended positioning** | Actual positioning | What the company's communication says, against the image that actually forms in customers' minds (the Nano). |
| **Loyal customer** | Profitable customer | A customer who returns only for freebies can have a negative lifetime value. |
| **Heuristic** | Bias | The mental shortcut, and the systematic error it produces. |
| **Anchoring** | The decoy | Anchoring: a first number sets the scale for an estimate. Decoy: a third, clearly inferior option makes another look good (popcorn tubs). |
| **Framing** | Positioning | Framing is how a company presents a fact in its own communication. The Nano's "cheapest car" image came from the media: a positioning problem. |
| **Availability** | Representativeness | What is easily recalled feels likely (a recent crash). What fits a stereotype feels likely (Linda, the white coat). |
| **33% more** | 33% off | 33% more is only 25% off. Compare price per unit. |
| **Left-digit effect** | 9-ending as a deal signal | Rs. 999 reads as nine hundred-something. Separately, a price ending in 9 signals an offer ($39 sold more than $34). |
| **Observation** | Ethnography | Ethnography is observation over a long period, living within the community. |
| **Habit** | Emotion | Reaching for the orange on the Tropicana pack is learned buying behaviour, not an emotional bond. |
| **High launch price** | Low launch price | High when rivals can copy fast or capacity is short. Low when habit makes early share decisive and capacity is ample. |
| **Low involvement** | High involvement | Low risk, little evaluation, brand switching. High economic, psychological or performance risk, careful comparison. |
| **Product development** | Market development | New product for the existing market, against the existing product for a new market. Both new: diversification. |
| **Psychographic** | Behavioural | A person's broad lifestyle, against how the person acts within this product category. |
| **Attractiveness** | Compatibility | How valuable the segment is to us, against whether we can serve it better than rivals. |
| **Weakness** | Threat | Internal to the company, against coming from outside it. |
| **Hierarchical clustering** | K-means clustering | Starts with every respondent alone and merges. Starts with a chosen number of clusters, k. |
| **Point of parity** | Point of difference | Gets you considered, against gets you chosen. |
| **Frame of reference** | Reason to believe | The category you are compared with (candy bar), against the proof of your claim (packed with peanuts). |
| **Repositioning** | Rebranding | A new image in customers' minds, against a new look, logo or name (Tropicana's pack). |
| **Repositioning** | Growth or extension | Adding products or segments while standing for the same thing is growth (Starbucks adding food). |
| **Line extension** | Category extension | Same category (a new flavour), against a new category (soap to lotion). |
| **Cannibalisation** | Dilution | The new product takes sales from your own existing product, against the new product weakens what the parent brand stands for. |
| **Endorser branding** | Sub-branding | The new name leads with a light endorsement, against the parent and the new name sharing emphasis (Honda City). |
| **House of brands** | Branded house | New names for new products (P&G, HUL), against one name on almost everything (Godrej, Dell, Virgin). |
| **Contribution** | Profit | Price minus variable cost, against what is left after fixed costs as well. |

## The module in twelve lines

1. Marketing starts from the customer's need, not from the factory: it creates, communicates, delivers and exchanges value, the four Ps, for customers and society.
2. A plan reads the situation with the 5Cs, chooses a strategy with STP and carries it out with the 4Ps: in that order, and with every P agreeing with the strategy.
3. A position can only be intended; the actual one forms in customers' minds, and changing it later is slow, costly and risky.
4. A customer is worth the discounted margins he will bring, weighted by the chance that he stays, less what it cost to win him.
5. Buyers are rational only within limits; they use shortcuts (anchoring, framing, availability, representativeness, the decoy, mental accounting) that a marketer can use and a consumer should watch for.
6. Research fails when it asks the wrong people, asks only what they say, or ignores how they actually buy; pair what people say with what they do.
7. Low-involvement goods are won by availability and recall; a copyable product is defended by service to the channel; growth options are mapped on the Ansoff matrix.
8. Segment on differences that change buying (start with the value customers seek, then profile who they are) and target segments that are both attractive and compatible.
9. Positioning is target plus differentiation: meet the points of parity, build on a point of difference, and state it as who, what and why.
10. Reaching a new segment or a new image must not cost the brand its existing customers or what it stands for.
11. A strong brand is a shortcut, a store of trust and a price premium; building one takes decades and four disciplines: cash, staying contemporary, breaking clutter, consistency.
12. Extend a brand only where its associations fit; choose how much of the parent's name to show; and prune the portfolio by contribution, rank, distinct positioning, growth and regional strength.
